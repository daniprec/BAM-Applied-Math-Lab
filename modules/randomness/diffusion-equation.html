The Diffusion Equation

Module 3: Modelling with Randomness

A single walker is unpredictable. The density of many walkers obeys a deterministic partial differential equation. This page derives it from the lattice walk, following the idea of (berg1993random?).

From the walk to a PDE

Let \(P(x, t)\) be the probability that a walker is at site \(x\) at time \(t\). An unbiased walker at \(x\) at time \(t + \tau\) came from \(x - \delta\) or from \(x + \delta\), each with probability \(1/2\):

\[ P(x, t + \tau) = \tfrac{1}{2} P(x - \delta, t) + \tfrac{1}{2} P(x + \delta, t). \]

Expand the left side to first order in \(\tau\) and the right side to second order in \(\delta\):

\[ \tau \frac{\partial P}{\partial t} = \frac{\delta^2}{2} \frac{\partial^2 P}{\partial x^2} + O(\delta^4) + O(\tau^2). \]

Now let \(\delta \to 0\) and \(\tau \to 0\) while the ratio \(D = \delta^2 / (2\tau)\) stays fixed. The result is the diffusion equation

\[ \frac{\partial P}{\partial t} = D \frac{\partial^2 P}{\partial x^2} . \tag{1}\]

The limit only makes sense with \(\delta^2 \propto \tau\). If you scale \(\delta \propto \tau\) instead, the right side vanishes and nothing spreads. This is the continuum version of the \(\sqrt{t}\) law from Mean Squared Displacement. The same equation, written for a concentration, is Fick’s second law (berg1993random?).

The heat kernel

For walkers that all start at \(x = 0\), \(P(x, 0) = \delta_{\text{Dirac}}(x)\), the solution on the whole line is

\[ P(x, t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi D t}} \exp\left(-\frac{x^2}{4Dt}\right), \tag{2}\]

a Gaussian with mean zero and variance \(2Dt\) (berg1993random?, Eq. 1.22). Its variance reproduces \(\langle x^2 \rangle = 2Dt\). The same Gaussian also follows from the binomial distribution of the walk when the number of steps is large, which is a special case of the central limit theorem.

Assumptions to remember

Equation 1 needs independent steps with finite variance and no bias. A bias adds a drift term, which gives diffusion with drift (berg1993random?). If the step variance is infinite, the limit is not a Gaussian at all; see Stable Distributions. In Module 4 the same Laplacian term appears in reaction-diffusion models.

Exercise

Check numerically that Equation 2 solves Equation 1. Evaluate heat_kernel on a grid at \(t = 10\) with \(D = 0.5\), approximate \(\partial_t P\) with a centred difference in time and \(\partial_{xx} P\) with the three-point stencil, and report the largest difference between the two sides.

Solution 
from amlab.random_walks import heat_kernel

D, t, h, dt = 0.5, 10.0, 0.01, 1e-4
xg = np.arange(-10, 10, h)
dPdt = (heat_kernel(xg, t + dt, D) - heat_kernel(xg, t - dt, D)) / (2 * dt)
P = heat_kernel(xg, t, D)
d2Pdx2 = (P[2:] - 2 * P[1:-1] + P[:-2]) / h**2
print(f"max |dP/dt - D d2P/dx2| = {np.max(np.abs(dPdt[1:-1] - D * d2Pdx2)):.2e}")
print(f"max |dP/dt| = {np.max(np.abs(dPdt)):.2e}")
max |dP/dt - D d2P/dx2| = 1.58e-08
max |dP/dt| = 6.31e-03

The residual is several orders of magnitude smaller than the terms themselves, as expected from discretization error alone.

## From the walk to a PDE

$$

$$

$$

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial t} = D \frac{\partial^2 P}{\partial x^2} .
$$ {#eq-diffusion}

The limit only makes sense with $\delta^2 \propto \tau$. If you scale $\delta \propto \tau$ instead, the right side vanishes and nothing spreads. ## The heat kernel

$$
P(x, t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi D t}} \exp\left(-\frac{x^2}{4Dt}\right),
$$ {#eq-heat-kernel}

a Gaussian with mean zero and variance $2Dt$ [@berg1993random, Eq. 1.22]. Its variance reproduces $\langle x^2 \rangle = 2Dt$. The same Gaussian also follows from the binomial distribution of the walk when the number of steps is large, which is a special case of the [central limit theorem](central-limit-theorem.qmd).

```{python}
#| label: fig-diffusion-histogram
#| fig-cap: "Positions of 20000 lattice walkers ($\\delta = \\tau = 1$, so $D = 1/2$) after 25, 100 and 400 steps, compared with the heat kernel. Bins have width $2\\delta$ because a walker after $n$ steps sits only on sites with the parity of $n$."
#| echo: false

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import numpy as np

from amlab.random_walks import heat_kernel, simulate_lattice_walk

rng = np.random.default_rng(17)
x = simulate_lattice_walk(n_walkers=20000, n_steps=400, rng=rng)
D = 0.5

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(7, 3.5))
for n, color in zip([25, 100, 400], ["C0", "C1", "C2"]):
    edges = np.arange(-81, 82, 2) + (n % 2)
    ax.hist(x[:, n], bins=edges, density=True, alpha=0.4, color=color, label=f"$t = {n}$")
    grid = np.linspace(-80, 80, 400)
    ax.plot(grid, heat_kernel(grid, n, D), color=color)
ax.set_xlabel("position $x$")
ax.set_ylabel("density")
ax.legend()
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()
```
