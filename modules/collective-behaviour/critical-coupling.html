Critical Coupling

Module 2: Synchronization and Collective Behaviour

This page summarizes Kuramoto’s self-consistency argument for the limit \(N \to \infty\), following Strogatz (2000, sec. 4). Work in the rotating frame, so \(g\) is symmetric about \(0\).

Steady states with constant \(r\)

Look for steady solutions in which \(r\) and \(\psi\) are constant. In the rotating frame, with \(g\) symmetric, you can choose the origin of phases so that \(\psi = 0\) (Strogatz 2000). Then the mean-field equation reads

\[ \dot\theta_i = \omega_i - K r \sin\theta_i. \tag{1}\]

Each oscillator is a nonuniform oscillator (Flows on the Circle) with \(a = Kr\). The population splits in two groups:

  • Locked oscillators, \(|\omega_i| \le Kr\), sit at a stable fixed point with \(\sin\theta_i = \omega_i/(Kr)\) and \(|\theta_i| \le \pi/2\).
  • Drifting oscillators, \(|\omega_i| > Kr\), circulate around the circle. Their stationary distribution is symmetric under \(\theta \to \theta + \pi\) together with \(\omega \to -\omega\), so they add nothing to \(r\) (Strogatz 2000).

Self-consistency

Only the locked oscillators contribute to \(r\). Using \(\omega = Kr\sin\theta\) to change variables,

\[ r = K r \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} \cos^2\theta \; g(Kr\sin\theta)\, d\theta. \tag{2}\]

\(r = 0\) always solves Equation 2: this is the incoherent state. A partially locked state with \(r > 0\) must satisfy

\[ 1 = K \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} \cos^2\theta \; g(Kr\sin\theta)\, d\theta. \tag{3}\]

Letting \(r \to 0^+\) and using \(\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\cos^2\theta\,d\theta = \pi/2\) gives the threshold where this branch starts:

\[ K_c = \frac{2}{\pi g(0)}. \tag{4}\]

This is Kuramoto’s formula for the critical coupling (Kuramoto 2003; Strogatz 2000).

Two cases you will use

Lorentzian frequencies. For \(g(\omega) = \gamma / [\pi(\gamma^2 + \omega^2)]\), Equation 4 gives \(K_c = 2\gamma\) and Equation 3 can be solved exactly:

\[ r = \sqrt{1 - \frac{K_c}{K}}, \qquad K \ge K_c. \tag{5}\]

Normal frequencies. For a normal density with standard deviation \(\sigma\), \(K_c = 2\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma/\pi = \sqrt{8/\pi}\,\sigma \approx 1.596\,\sigma\). There is no closed form for \(r(K)\), so you solve Equation 3 numerically.

Square-root onset

For any smooth \(g\) with \(g''(0) < 0\), expanding Equation 3 for small \(r\) gives

\[ r \approx \sqrt{\frac{16}{\pi K_c^3}}\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{-g''(0)}}, \qquad \mu = \frac{K - K_c}{K_c}, \tag{6}\]

so \(r\) grows like \(\sqrt{K - K_c}\) just above threshold (Strogatz 2000). The transition from incoherence to partial locking is continuous. The same square-root law appears at a supercritical pitchfork bifurcation.

Measure the transition

The simulation below integrates the mean-field equations \(\dot\theta_i = \omega_i + K r \sin(\psi - \theta_i)\) for \(N\) oscillators, with \(r\) and \(\psi\) recomputed at every step. Blue dots are locked (\(|\omega_i| \le Kr\)), red ones drift. The bottom panel compares your measurements with the \(N \to \infty\) theory of this page.

  1. Set \(K = 1\), below \(K_c \approx 1.60\). Is \(r\) exactly zero? Compare \(N = 50\) with \(N = 1000\), and relate the size of the fluctuations to \(N^{-1/2}\).
  2. Raise \(K\) just past \(K_c\). A cluster forms. Watch the percentage of locked oscillators grow as you increase \(K\).
  3. Press Sweep K automatically.   4. Switch to the Lorentzian density. Check \(K_c = 2\) and the exact law \(r = \sqrt{1 - K_c/K}\) from Equation 5.

What the argument does not show

Kuramoto’s argument finds the steady states. It does not prove which of them are stable, nor that the population approaches them. These questions were settled only by much later work, which Strogatz (2000) reviews. Finite \(N\) adds fluctuations of order \(N^{-1/2}\) on top of these curves (Finite-Size Effects).

NoteExercise

Check that Equation 6 agrees with the Lorentzian result Equation 5 near \(K_c\).

TipSolution

For \(\gamma = 1\), \(g(0) = 1/\pi\), \(K_c = 2\) and \(g''(0) = -2/\pi\). Then Equation 6 gives \(r \approx \sqrt{16/(8\pi)}\,\sqrt{\mu\pi/2} = \sqrt{\mu}\). The exact result is \(r = \sqrt{(K - K_c)/K} = \sqrt{\mu K_c/K}\), which tends to \(\sqrt{\mu}\) as \(K \to K_c\).

NoteExercise

Your frequencies are normal with \(\sigma = 0.5\). What is \(K_c\)? What happens to \(K_c\) if you double the spread of frequencies?

TipSolution

\(K_c = \sqrt{8/\pi}\times 0.5 \approx 0.80\). Since \(g(0) \propto 1/\sigma\), \(K_c\) is proportional to \(\sigma\). Doubling the spread doubles \(K_c\): a more diverse population needs stronger coupling to synchronize.

$$

```{python}
#| label: fig-critical-coupling
#| fig-cap: "Order parameter in the limit $N \\to \\infty$. Solid: exact Lorentzian result @eq-r-lorentz with $\\gamma = 1$ ($K_c = 2$). Dashed: near-onset approximation @eq-r-onset for a normal density with $\\sigma = 1$ ($K_c \\approx 1.60$), valid only close to $K_c$."
#| fig-width: 6
#| fig-height: 3.5
#| echo: false
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

from amlab.synchronization.kuramoto_sweep import critical_coupling

k = np.linspace(0, 6, 600)

kc_lor = critical_coupling(1 / np.pi)
r_lor = np.where(k > kc_lor, np.sqrt(np.clip(1 - kc_lor / np.maximum(k, 1e-12), 0, None)), 0.0)

sigma = 1.0
g0 = 1 / (np.sqrt(2 * np.pi) * sigma)
g2 = -g0 / sigma**2
kc_norm = critical_coupling(g0)
mu = (k - kc_norm) / kc_norm
r_norm = np.where(k > kc_norm, np.sqrt(16 / (np.pi * kc_norm**3) * np.clip(mu, 0, None) / (-g2)), 0.0)
mask = (k < kc_norm) | (r_norm < 0.8)

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(6, 3.5))
ax.plot(k, r_lor, label=r"Lorentzian, $\gamma = 1$")
ax.plot(k[mask], r_norm[mask], "--", label=r"Normal, $\sigma = 1$ (near onset)")
ax.axvline(kc_lor, color="C0", linewidth=0.8, linestyle=":")
ax.axvline(kc_norm, color="C1", linewidth=0.8, linestyle=":")
ax.set_xlabel("Coupling strength K")
ax.set_ylabel(r"$r_\infty$")
ax.set_ylim(0, 1)
ax.legend()
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()
```
